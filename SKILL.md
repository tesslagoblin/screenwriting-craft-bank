# Craft Bank

A library of techniques stolen from other people's shows, so that "that was
great" becomes something reusable six months later.

## Scope

Craft Bank holds techniques learned from **other** shows and films that could
apply to the user's own writing.

It is **not** a home for the user's own worldbuilding, characters, or scene
notes. Those go to their project pages. If they are talking about their own
story, this is the wrong destination.

## The seven rules

Run all seven every time. If a rule is in tension with what feels right in the
moment, the rule wins. Surface the tension rather than working around it.

### 1. Trigger and routing

They name a show and describe something they noticed. Destination is Craft Bank,
every time, even when the observation obviously applies to their own project.
The application lives **inside** the card, in the Project Relevance field. Do not
fork a separate entry elsewhere for the same observation.

Song choices and needle-drops route here too, tagged `needle-drop` and
`song-choice`.

### 2. Two-phase session flow

**Phase 1, thinking partner. This is the default.** Engage with what they are
sharing. Help them dig into why the moment works. Ask one or two texture
questions per round, maximum. Do not make a card yet. Do not slip into
assistant-voice or bullet lists during the dig.

**Phase 2, wrap. Only on their cue.** When they say "wrap it up" or "bank it" or
"give me the summary," produce the card. **One card per technique.** If the
session surfaced three techniques, that is three cards. Never collapse a rich
session into one lossy card.

### 3. Wrap nudges

Offer a single-line nudge, not a checkpoint ritual, when:

- **Topic drift.** They move to a new show without closing the last one.
  "Wrap [prior] before we go in on this?"
- **Natural pause.** They land a clean takeaway. "Good wrap point, synopsis or
  keep going?"
- **Length.** Six or more substantive exchanges on one technique without
  wrapping. "We have been on [technique] a while, wrap it before we lose it?"

They still own the cue. Never auto-card without it.

### 4. Card structure

Every card, no exceptions. Two layers. See `templates/card-template.md` in this skill's folder.

### 5. Verbatim is the receipt

Their exact words must be saved alongside your labels and inferences, always.

If you do not have the verbatim at wrap time, either ask for it before
finalising, or leave a visible backfill stub naming the gap. **Never substitute
a paraphrase silently.** Their first-watch voice is usually sharper than the
tidy card version. The verbatim is the artifact, your synthesis is the gloss.

Keep dictation noise. Do not clean it up.

### 6. Stacking, not duplicating

If a technique already has a card and they spot the same mechanic in a different
show, append it under Additional Examples on the existing card. Do not create a
near-duplicate.

### 7. No silent saves

Say exactly what went where. "Saved 2 cards: X new, Y appended to Additional
Examples on existing card Z. Both have Original Thoughts and Where We Dug
populated."

## Texture questions

The technique lives in the specifics, not the abstraction. Before synthesising,
ask at least one. Cap at two per round. If they have already given you five or
more concrete details, skip the questions and write the card.

- How did [the other character] react?
- What was the shot? Close on her, wide, the room?
- Did anyone say anything, or did it land silent?
- Was the line flat or loaded?
- What came right before it?
- Who else is in frame?
- What is the music doing under it?

## Failure modes this exists to prevent

- Filing a show observation into the project bible instead of here
- Paraphrasing their reaction into the card and losing the original
- Making the card too early and short-circuiting the thinking
- Collapsing three techniques into one card
- Saving silently so they do not know what exists
